How it works

  1. Describe what you sell

    Your offers and the words clients use for them, a short introduction, and a few sentences of proof: work you've shipped, as you'd say it. That's everything a message will ever say about you.

  2. Pick where your clients ask

    Turn on the sources that fit: hiring threads, subreddits, launch sites, local businesses with weak websites, or any job board's RSS feed. Each is read through its public API or feed.

  3. Read the morning digest

    Every day at your hour, new matches arrive in your inbox, by email and on Slack or Discord if you like, best fit first. Each shows why it scored what it did and what one look at their homepage showed.

  4. Send it yourself

    Edit the draft, copy it, reply where they posted. Mark it sent, replied or won, and export everything to CSV whenever you want.

Where it looks

Hacker News

Contract and freelance posts in the monthly hiring thread, and Show HN launches that match your phrases.

Reddit

New posts asking for help or someone to hire, in the subreddits you choose.

RemoteOK

Contract and part-time remote roles that name your offers or tools.

Product Hunt

New launches matching your phrases, with the maker and their site.

Google Maps

Established local businesses with no website, or one that's broken or thin.

Any RSS feed

Job boards, forum tags, saved searches: if it has a feed, it's a source.

What it will never do

  • Send a message for you. Every message is read and sent by a person. No cold-email blasts, no auto-DMs.
  • Make up claims. A draft says only your introduction and proof, word for word. The AI writes one sentence about what the lead needs, and drafts that would claim anything else fall back to plain text.
  • Scrape behind a login. Sources are public APIs and feeds, read the way their owners allow, and every lead links back to the original post.
  • Probe anyone's site. It loads a lead's homepage once, like any visitor, and notes only what that showed.

Run it yourself

Leadkiln is open source under AGPL-3.0. The command line runs a search from a config file and writes the leads to Markdown and JSON; the hosted app runs on Cloudflare Workers. The code is on GitHub.
